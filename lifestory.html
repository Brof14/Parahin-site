My story

At 14, I launched ByMeVPN. Back then, it was pretty simple: a VPN server, a Telegram bot, and an idea to build a service other people could use.

Then the first users arrived. With them came payments, subscriptions, support requests, connection issues, and plenty of things I'd never thought about at the start.

It turns out that writing code that works and building a business that works are two very different things.

I grew up in a small village in Altai, Russia. I started programming at 12 and learned most of it on my own. I've always wanted to build something of my own — not just study technology, but use it to bring my ideas to life.

ByMeVPN was the first time that went beyond experiments. Real people started using the service, and I gradually moved from a Telegram bot to developing my own infrastructure and apps for Android, Windows, and Linux.

Not everything went smoothly. There were times when the app said the VPN was connected, even though the connection wasn't actually working. Sometimes fixing one bug caused another. You learn quickly that users don't care how complicated the system behind a product is. They just need it to work.

These days, I do a significant part of my development work with AI. It makes the path from idea to implementation much faster, but it doesn't remove the need to understand the architecture, make decisions, and take responsibility for the result.

Over time, I've become increasingly interested in the other side of these projects: business. How do you find customers, make the economics work, compete, and scale? That's also where my interest in financial markets, investing, and how companies are built and grow comes from.

I don't plan to stop at one VPN service. ByMeVPN became my first commercial product. My next goal is to build technology companies, enter international markets, and eventually establish an investment business of my own.
