My story

At 14, I launched ByMeVPN. The first version was a VPN server and a Telegram bot. Then real users showed up. It didn't take long to realize that launching a service and keeping it working every day are two very different jobs.

Payments, subscriptions, support, outages, fixes — none of that shows up when you're coding for yourself. One Windows build even said “Connected” while the internet wasn't working. To the person using it, that's not “almost done.” It's broken.

I grew up in a small village in Altai, Russia. I started coding at 12, mostly learning online and by building things on my own. ByMeVPN was the first project that went beyond an experiment.

The project grew beyond the bot: infrastructure, payments and subscriptions, and apps for Android, Windows, and Linux that I'm still developing. Working on it pushed me to think beyond the code.

AI helps me research unfamiliar problems, write and test code, and automate repetitive work. It speeds things up, but I'm still responsible for what ships.

I'm just as interested in the business side: why people choose one product over another, where customers come from, what it costs to serve them, and how a company grows. That's also what drew me to financial markets and investing.

ByMeVPN is my first commercial project, not my last. I want to build technology companies for global markets and, eventually, invest in others.
